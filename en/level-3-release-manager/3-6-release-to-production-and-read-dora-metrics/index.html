Lab 3.6 - Release to production and read your DORA metrics¶

Level: 3 Release Manager

Time: ~1 h (observed: median 1 h 01, 9 learners, fastest 20 min)

You will: release to production, and then measure whether your pipeline is any good.

The situation¶

UAT signed off. The release goes to production this evening.

This is the same mechanism as Lab 3.5, twice: uat into preprod, then preprod into main. With one difference that is not technical: if you get it wrong, real people cannot do their jobs tomorrow. Everything in this lab that looks like ceremony is there because somebody skipped it once.

Before you start¶

  • Lab 3.5 finished: uat carries the release and the testers signed it off
  • helios-preprod and helios-prod connected, seeded, and configured as the preprod and main orgs in Lab 3.1
  • JWT authentication working for preprod and main

Steps¶

1. Check the three things that are worth checking¶

Goal three answers before you create anything: UAT signed off, the manual steps known, production where you think it is.

Refresher Lab 3.5

Before creating anything:

Do

  1. One: is UAT genuinely signed off? Not "the deployment was green". Somebody tested it and said yes. On this project that person is you, and you did it in Lab 3.5 step 6
  2. Two: what manual steps will this carry? Look at the deployment actions of the stories going out
  3. Three: is production where you think it is? Open helios-prod and look

Why

A manual step in production is something you will do, live, in front of nobody, at whatever time the release is. Know about it now.

On a real project, admins change production by hand between two releases, and Lab 3.7 is about exactly that. Assume nothing.

You should now see

  • The manual steps of the release, known before you create anything
  • helios-prod carrying what the course seeded into it and nothing else yet

2. Rehearse in preprod¶

Goal the release deployed to helios-preprod and checked there, before production.

Refresher Lab 3.5 Lab 3.1

Do

  1. Click the + PR chip on the arrow from uat to preprod, in the DevOps Pipeline diagram, the same way you created the promotion in Lab 3.5. GitHub opens on the Pull Request from uat into preprod

  2. Title it plainly, then click Create pull request:

    Promotion 2026-09 to preprod

  3. Look at the first JWT login of the course once: open the check from Checks, expand Login & Simulate deployment, and look for sf org login jwt

  4. Its first check stops red, on purpose. Do the click of the deliverability step of US-026 in helios-preprod
  5. Tick its box in the sfdx-hardis comment (or Mark as done in preprod in the VS Code Deployment Actions tab), then Re-run all jobs on the check. It goes green
  6. Read the check, merge, and watch the Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run on preprod
  7. Open helios-preprod and do the checks of step 6 there first

Why

Its check job is the first one to log into helios-preprod, and it does so with the key and the secrets of Lab 3.1. The sf org login jwt line, and the job carrying on past it, are your preprod key working, even on the first run, which stops red further down for another reason.

That reason is the one of the promotion to uat in Lab 3.5 step 4: the deliverability step of US-026 runs before the deployment, and nobody has done it in helios-preprod.

This is what preprod is for. It holds what production holds, nobody works in it, and a release that deploys there cleanly and behaves has very little left to surprise you with in production. A release that fails here has cost you nothing.

You should now see

  • sf org login jwt in the log of the check, and the job carrying on past it
  • A green Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run on preprod, and the release behaving in helios-preprod

3. Create the production Pull Request¶

Goal the production Pull Request from preprod into main, with a green check to read.

Do

  1. Click the + PR chip on the arrow from preprod to main, from preprod into main

  2. Title it plainly, then click Create pull request:

    Release 2026-09 to production

  3. In the log of its check job, look for sf org login jwt, as in step 2

  4. Its first check stops red for the same reason as in step 2. Do the click in production, tick the box, and click Re-run all jobs

Why

Its check job is the first JWT login into helios-prod. And helios-prod is the last org where nobody has done the deliverability step.

You should now see

  • The green check that follows the re-run, ready to read

4. Read the check like it matters¶

Goal you know whether this release deletes anything in production, and how long it will take.

Refresher Lab 3.2

Do

  1. When the check finishes, read the sfdx-hardis comment the way Lab 3.2 taught
  2. Add the two questions of the table below, which only apply to production
Question Where to look
Does it delete anything? The deleted figure in the Metadata row, shown only when something goes. A deletion in production is permanent and takes data with it
How long will it take? The check duration is a reasonable estimate. If it is 40 minutes, that is 40 minutes during which the org is being modified

Careful

If that deleted figure is there and you were not expecting it, stop. The comment will not tell you what is going: manifest/destructiveChanges.xml and the diff will. Find out what it is and who intended it.

Why

Stopping on an unexpected deletion is not being careful, that is the job.

You should now see

  • Your answer to both questions: a deleted figure or none, and the duration of the check

5. Merge, and stay¶

Goal a green deployment on main, watched to its end, and its post-deployment manual steps done.

Refresher Lab 3.5

Do

  1. Merge. The Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run starts, this time on main
  2. Watch it
  3. When it finishes, do the post-deployment manual steps, if any, then check the org

Why

Not because you can do anything while it runs, but because knowing whether it failed at minute two or minute thirty-five changes what you do next.

The pre-deployment ones belong before the merge: do them, tick their boxes in the check comment, then merge, and the deployment job records them, as in Lab 3.5.

You should now see

  • A green Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run on main

6. Verify in production¶

Goal the two stories working in helios-prod, and something that already worked still working.

Do

  1. Open Orgs Manager and find helios-prod by its alias (2)

  2. Check it says Connected (3), then Open from the actions menu at the end of its row. A disconnected row offers Reconnect in that same menu; Add Org (1) is for an org the table does not have

  3. Then check, the same as UAT and with more care, that the two stories work

  4. Check something that was already working still works: open an installation, check the timeline component, save a record

Why

That last check exists because the most common production incident after a release is not the new feature failing. It is an old one.

You should now see

  • The two stories working in helios-prod
  • An installation that opens, its timeline component, and a record that saves

7. Now measure the pipeline¶

Goal the DORA metrics of your pipeline on screen, and their frame, flow and five numbers read.

Refresher Lab 3.1 Lab 1.2

You have shipped. The question a release manager gets asked next is "how are we doing", and it deserves a better answer than a feeling.

Do

  1. Open the DevOps Pipeline panel, click the gear (1) at the top right, and choose Generate DORA Metrics (2)

  2. A DORA metrics tab opens and says, for a few seconds, that it is reading deployment jobs and Pull Requests from GitHub. It lands on its Overview tab

  3. Read the frame before the numbers:

    • The period (1) is Last 90 days unless you pick 30, 180 or 365
    • The line under the title (2) names the repository and the dates of the period, then says where the data of a branch starts when that is more than a week after the period does. Here it says so for all four branches: your pipeline is younger than the period it is measured over
    • Pipeline flow (3) follows your User Stories from one org to the next. You read it in a moment, below
    • Needs attention (4) is there only when sfdx-hardis has something to point at, each line with the branch it is about and, when there is something to open, an Open button. It knows five cases: the last deployment job of a branch failed, Pull Requests have been open for more than 7 days, the failure rate is above 10 % and rose by 5 points since the period before, nothing was deployed for 30 days, User Stories have been waiting in an org for more than 30 days. If you sent US-020 back more than a week ago, it is on that list, and rightly so: a Pull Request nobody merges and nobody closes is a decision somebody is not making
    • The table (5) holds one row per major org, in the order of the pipeline, with main marked production. A row opens the tab of its branch, the one of main being (6)

  4. Read Pipeline flow from left to right:

    • Each box is an org of the pipeline. It says how many User Stories are in that org and went no further, and how long the oldest has been there. Yours all read Nothing waiting: you have just released everything. On a real project this is where a release manager looks first, and a click on a box lists the User Stories waiting in it
    • Between two boxes, the arrow carries the median wait: the days between the deployment of a User Story in one org and its deployment in the next. Yours waited 4 d in integration, 0.8 d in uat and 0.2 d in preprod. The longest of them is marked Longest wait
    • Time to production (median), on the right, is the whole trip: from the creation of the Pull Request of a User Story to the end of the job that deployed it to production. 5.7 d, High, over the ten User Stories you delivered
    • Slowest changes to reach production opens the list behind that number, each User Story with the day it reached each org. The slowest is the first one you ever merged: it waited for the pipeline to exist

  5. Read the five numbers each row carries, with the table below. Each one is rated Elite, High, Medium or Low, on the same scale for every branch, or reads No data

Column What it actually counts What good looks like
Deployments per week Successful runs of the deployment job of the branch, divided by the length of the period Weekly is fine. Quarterly means every release is enormous
Lead time (median) Per User Story that reached the org: the creation of its Pull Request, to its deployment there Days, not weeks. A long lead time means work is sitting somewhere
Failure rate Failed deployment jobs, divided by failed plus succeeded. A cancelled job is in neither Below 15 %. Above that, the check is not catching what it should
Recovery (median) Hours from a failed deployment job to the next one that succeeds on the same branch Hours
Rework Pull Requests from a fix branch, or successes within a day of a failure, whichever is the larger Low. Read the note below before you expect Lab 3.7 to move it

Why

You used Configure Pipeline Settings and Add or Reconnect a Major Org of the same menu in Lab 3.1. Configure Org Initialization and Configure Replacement Rules are two settings this course does not use: Lab 3.1 says what they are for.

The sentence the tab shows while it loads is the whole design. Nothing is asked of any org, so there is no default org to set first, and two people on the same project read the same numbers. What the panel needs is the connection to GitHub you gave the extension in Lab 1.2.

Pipeline flow answers the question the table cannot: how long does a User Story take to reach production, and where does it wait. The figures quoted above are the ones of the picture, a learner who spread the course over a week: yours follow the days your own course took and the number of User Stories you delivered, and a course done in one sitting reads hours, not days.

A User Story is followed from the Pull Requests alone. Merging integration into uat carries every User Story that was in integration at that moment, and the days are counted from there.

The column between the first two, named after the number of weeks in the period, draws the deployments of the branch week by week.

Two of those are not what the names suggest, and it is worth knowing which. Failure rate is the failure rate of a job: a release that deployed green and broke production on Tuesday does not appear in it. Recovery is the gap between a red deployment job and the next green one, not between an incident and its fix. They measure your pipeline, not your org. And the pipeline is all they see: a deployment made around it, a change set or the seeding Set up one of my training orgs did, is counted nowhere.

You should now see

  • The Overview tab with the Pipeline flow, every box reading Nothing waiting
  • One row per major org, main marked production

8. Read what is there, and know what is missing¶

Goal each number of the production tab read for what it really says, and your history found in integration.

Refresher Lab 3.3

Do

  1. Click the main row, or the main (production) tab (1)

  2. Read the tab: the five numbers as tiles (2), the deployments of each week as bars, green for succeeded and red for failed (3), the failed deployments with the time each took to recover (4), and the supplementary metrics (5)

  3. Scroll down for the rest of those, and for two lists that open on a click: the Pull Requests that took more than 7 days to merge, and the hotfix Pull Requests

  4. Read it number by number, because each one is true and none of them means what a hurried reader would take from it:

    • Deployments: 0.1 / week, Low. One successful job, divided by a period of 90 days that was empty until this week. The rating is about the period, not about you. Pick Last 30 days and the same single deployment weighs three times as much
    • Lead time (median): 5.7 d, High. The time your User Stories took to reach production, each one from the creation of its own Pull Request: the number Time to production showed on the Overview. Under it, Pull Requests of this branch alone: 0.1 d is the life of the release Pull Request, from the + PR chip to the end of its deployment job. A couple of hours, and it would rate Elite. Quoting that one as your lead time would be flattering and wrong: the release was quick, the User Stories it carried had been waiting for days
    • Failure rate: 0 %, Elite, and Rework: 0 %, Elite, over one deployment. One red job next week and the first reads 50 %
    • Recovery (median): No data. Nothing failed on main, so there is nothing to recover from. No data is the honest answer, not an error

  5. Now open the integration tab

Why

You have shipped once, and this tab says exactly that.

The picture is one learner's evening, and yours will differ in the detail: the dates, a second job on main if you merged twice. The shape will not.

integration is where your history is. Over ten deployments, the single failure of Lab 3.3 is ten points of failure rate. Small numbers move in big steps.

That is the honest version of this step, and it is also the point. DORA metrics on a pipeline that has released once are an empty baseline. They become useful at the fourth or fifth release, when the numbers have somewhere to move from: the panel then draws an arrow next to each one and prints its value over the previous period. Today the period before is empty, so there is no arrow.

You should now see

  • On main, one deployment, and no arrow next to any number
  • On integration, one deployment for every Pull Request merged since you started, and the red job of Lab 3.3 in Failed deployments, with the time until the next green job

9. Keep the baseline¶

Goal a DORA report in docs/dora/, the baseline Lab 3.11 compares the next one with.

Do

  1. Open Export (1) and choose Markdown report (2)

  2. When the command panel has finished, its DORA Metrics Report button opens the file

  3. Keep the file where it is

Careful

The file is named after the day, so a second export on the same day replaces it: if you go on to Lab 3.11 today, note the numbers of integration and main first, or copy the file under another name.

Why

The numbers on screen are read again each time, so the baseline has to be a file. The other entry, PDF report, writes a PDF as well.

The report is a file, docs/dora/dora-report-<date>.md, and Check my work looks for it. It is rebuilt from the jobs and the Pull Requests whenever you export again, so nothing is committed, and Lab 3.11 compares the next one with this one.

Under the hood: where the DORA numbers come from

The panel ran:

sf hardis:doc:dora-report --agent --data-only --compare --period 90 --json

and Export ran the same command without those flags, in the command panel:

sf hardis:doc:dora-report --period 90

It has one source, the git provider, which is the thing to know about it. Until sfdx-hardis 8.16 the deployments were read from the org; they no longer are, and the command does not log into any org:

  • A deployment is a run of the deployment job of a major branch, the one a merge starts. On GitHub that is a run of the Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) workflow triggered by a push to the branch. Runs that no merge started, a scheduled one for instance, are not deployments
  • A validation is the deployment check of a Pull Request, here a run of Simulate Deployment (sfdx-hardis). The Validation success rate of the supplementary metrics is built on them
  • The Pull Requests merged into each major branch, and the ones still open

The jobs are told apart by their name. In a project that renamed them, when no run of a branch is named as a deployment, every run a push to that branch started counts as one.

It measures every major branch, which means every config/branches/.sfdx-hardis.<branch>.yml of the project, when a person runs it. In a CI job it measures the branch of the job, and --branches names them explicitly. Then, per branch:

  • A merge is deployed by the successful job of its merge commit, or, when the provider does not say which one that is, by the first successful job started within 14 days of the merge. With neither, the merge date stands for the deployment
  • Lead time is counted per User Story: from the creation of its Pull Request to its deployment in the org of the tab, whatever merge brought it there. The line under it keeps the older reading, the Pull Requests merged into that branch alone, which is also what a notification to Grafana carries
  • Rework takes the larger of two counts, the Pull Requests whose branch starts with hotfix, fix or bugfix, and the successful jobs that follow a failed one within 24 hours, and divides it by the deployments
  • A cancelled job is counted apart and enters no rate
  • A job run again on the same commit replaces its previous result on some providers. On GitHub, Re-run all jobs keeps the run and its last result, so the checks that stopped red on purpose in steps 2 and 3 count as green

How a User Story is followed. No git history is read, only the Pull Requests:

  • A Pull Request from a major branch into the next one (uat into preprod) carries every User Story that was in its source branch when it was merged
  • A promotion Pull Request, the selective kind of Lab 3.10, carries the Pull Requests listed in its description and nothing else
  • A hotfix merged straight into preprod is counted from preprod. The merge that brings it back down to integration does not send it through the pipeline a second time
  • A change cherry-picked by hand is not followed, and neither is one merged more than 90 days before the period

The steps between the orgs are the mergeTargets of each config/branches/.sfdx-hardis.<branch>.yml.

One thing to check on any project. Rework recognises a corrective change by its branch name. This project names its fix branches fix/, so the hotfix you ship in Lab 3.7 counts, in the Rework of preprod, the branch it is merged into. Rename that prefix to something else and the metric quietly reads zero, with no warning. A number built on branch names is only as good as the naming convention, which is worth checking before quoting one at anybody.

What the three flags of the panel do:

  • --data-only returns the numbers and writes nothing: no report, no CSV, no notification. The panel draws that answer as it comes and computes nothing itself, so the panel and the report cannot disagree
  • --compare also computes the period of the same length just before, which is where the arrows and the previous values come from, and what lets a branch say when its data starts
  • --period is the list at the top of the panel

Finished jobs do not change, so sfdx-hardis keeps them in ~/.sfdx/sfdx-hardis-cache/dora, one file per repository, and asks the provider only for the jobs of the last two days and the new ones. Refresh adds --refresh, which reads everything again. The panel also keeps its last answer, to have something to show while the next one is being read.

Export writes the report in hardis-report/, copies it to docs/dora/, and adds a CSV of the jobs behind the numbers. A run on one branch also sends a notification, to Slack, Teams or Grafana when one is configured; a run on your four branches sends none.

Without a git provider the command fails: there is no fallback on git log any more, and no number is better than a wrong one. It also fails, saying which right is missing, when the token cannot read the CI jobs. On GitHub that right is Actions: Read.

The numbers are honest in a way a dashboard somebody fills in by hand never is. Nobody can improve deployment frequency by editing a spreadsheet. They are also narrower than the DORA names suggest, and a release manager quoting them should know which part they cover.

Command documentation: hardis:doc:dora-report

You should now see

  • The command panel writing the report for the same period: a summary of every branch, then a chapter per branch
  • docs/dora/dora-report-<date>.md on your disk

What you should see¶

  • preprod and main carrying the release
  • A green Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run on preprod, then one on main
  • The stories working in helios-prod
  • The DORA metrics panel with a row for each of your four branches, and one deployment on main
  • A DORA report in docs/dora/
If it goes wrong

The Pull Request from uat into preprod has a conflict in config/.sfdx-hardis.yml. Two things together cause it. Your preprod came from a main newer than the course your integration started from (Sync fork on GitHub moves main), and a course update reached integration as a Squash and merge instead of the merge commit Update my course makes. Git then cannot see that both branches carry the same course changes, and it stops where your pipeline settings sit next to a line the course changed. Nothing is lost: uat holds the right file. Bring preprod into uat through a branch of its own, from the Command Palette of VS Code:

  1. Git: Fetch
  2. Git: Create Branch From..., pick origin/uat, and name it fix/merge-preprod-into-uat
  3. Git: Merge..., and pick origin/preprod, under remote branches
  4. Open config/.sfdx-hardis.yml under Merge Changes, click Accept Current Change above each conflict, save, click + on its row, then Commit. Current is uat, which already has the course changes and your settings
  5. Publish Branch, then on GitHub open its Pull Request into uat, wait for the checks, and merge it with a merge commit (Create a merge commit, under the arrow of the green button), never a squash: a squash brings the conflict back

The Pull Request into preprod then has no conflict. Its check runs again and stops on the deliverability step, as step 2 says.

The deployment to production fails on a component that worked in preprod. Production has drifted, or has something preprod does not: an extra validation rule, a record type, real data that violates a new constraint. Read the error. This is the single most common production deployment failure, and it is the argument for keeping preprod as close to production as you can.

The deployment half-succeeded. Salesforce deployments are atomic per deployment, so this usually means a post-deploy action failed after a successful deployment. The metadata is in, the action is not. Re-run the action, do not re-run the deployment.

The DORA metrics panel says "Upgrade sfdx-hardis". The panel needs sfdx-hardis 8.17.0 or later. Click Open Setup, upgrade sfdx-hardis there, then Try Again.

The panel says "Connect to your git provider". The extension has no connection to GitHub in this VS Code. Click Connect to Git and sign in, as in Lab 1.2. The panel reads the numbers as soon as it is connected.

The panel says "DORA metrics could not be read". The message under that title is the one of sfdx-hardis, and it names the cause. The one to know on a real project says the git provider token cannot read the CI jobs: on GitHub, the token lacks Actions: Read on the repository. Fix the token, then click Try Again.

A number reads "No data". Nothing to measure, which is not an error: Recovery on a branch where no deployment failed, or every number of a branch that was not deployed in the period.

The release you just merged is not in the numbers. Only finished jobs are counted. Wait for the Process Deployment (sfdx-hardis) run to end, then click Refresh.

Check my work says no DORA report was exported. Reading the panel writes no file. Do step 9: Export, then Markdown report.
